Light-theme redesign — four directions

Standalone mockups of the Home and TimeSheet pages in four distinct visual registers, for choosing a direction before any SCSS is rewritten.

Product GSR Timesheet Portal Stage Direction selection — mockups only Viewport Desktop, ~1440px Data Raj Kumar (GSR-001), August 2026

How to read these

All four directions show identical content and identical data — the same sixteen August 2026 entries, the same holiday list, the same photos. Anything that differs between them is a design decision, not a content one, so the comparison is like-for-like.

All four are light-background, anchored on the navy sampled from the GSR logo (#183078), and buildable with the MUI components the app already uses — DatePicker, Table, Pagination, Tooltip. None of them requires a different component library.

Look at the TimeSheet page first. It is the screen people actually live in, it is where density, table legibility and the four day types get stress-tested, and it is where the four directions genuinely diverge. The Home page mostly tests how each direction handles photography.

A

Corporate calm

Reference register: Zoho People, BambooHR, greytHR, Darwinbox

White cards on a light cool ground, generous whitespace, navy section headers, hairline borders, almost no shadow. The least dense of the four and the most conventional.

The idea: nothing surprising — an HR administrator who used Zoho yesterday knows where everything is.

B

Modern product

Reference register: Linear (light), Attio, Height, Vercel

Tight 8px grid, crisp 1px borders, small radii, rationed colour, compact controls. Typography carries the hierarchy instead of spacing, so noticeably more fits on screen.

The idea: density without noise — clarity bought with typography rather than whitespace.

TimeSheet page → Home page →
C

Warm and human

Reference register: modern people/HR products — warm neutrals, real photography

Warm cream ground and warm greys instead of cool blue-greys, softer radii, a friendlier type pairing, and photography given real space. Navy stays the brand anchor, paired with a warm secondary for the social content.

The idea: this is a people product, not a spreadsheet — birthdays and faces are the point, not widgets.

TimeSheet page → Home page →
D

Structured / editorial

Reference register: editorial and institutional layout — annual reports, broadsheets

Rules and typographic hierarchy instead of cards, navy used structurally as a masthead band and table rules, a visible alignment grid, and high information density handled through separation rather than padding.

The idea: rules, not boxes — a serious internal tool that reads like a well-set document.
